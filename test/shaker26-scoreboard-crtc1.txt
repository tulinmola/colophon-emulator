What Longshot's Shaker 2.6 said about this machine, built as a type 1
CRTC, module by module and in the order each module's own menu prints.

Built as, and nearly no more: the chip answers a program asking what it
is, and of its own timing keeps only what the head of crtc.h names. The
rest is a type 0's, and most of what is wrong below is that, and is the
work rather than a fault.

70 groups run, 12 left to another CRTC type.
10 of them printed self-graded lines, 89 in all: 89 agree with Longshot's
silicon and 0 do not. That is 100% of what this reader can score, and no
measure at all of the other 60 groups.

A group stands as "recorded, ungraded" when it drew a screen of its own but
said what it had to say in a picture or a legend rather than in words
this reader can score, or because it names silicon's value only where the
machine differs from it. A group can also be graded in part: where a row
carries more than one test, the one whose value stands nearest the
expectation is the one scored. A group that got no further than its
module's menu stands as "showed only the menu": it was run and kept, not
skipped. A line marked ! is a test this machine failed: either the
module said so, or the machine's value differs from the one Longshot's
silicon produced. A module can also state a failure and mean it for
another type: where a line hands the passing outcome to CRTC types this
machine is not one of, the failure it marks is the answer asked for, and
the line stands unmarked; a pass it hands to those other types is marked
as the failure it is. A few lines here are graded against a title
rather than against anything standing on them: their group puts silicon's
value on a title and the machine's on the screen rows beneath it, so what
they were measured against stands on the screen they came from rather
than on the line copied out of it. Only module D's (R) is graded that
way, under the title "TST COMP C4/R7 ACTIVE DURING VSYNC FOR DEADLOCK
(EXP #5F)".

Not every difference here is this machine's doing. What a module prints
depends on the phase it booted on, and at least one group has been measured
printing a graded line on one phase and none on another with the emulator
altered in no way at all. When a line leaves this record, rule the phase out
before believing the loss.

Nor is every line here one the machine drew once. A screen read while it
was being redrawn is spliced from the two halves, and a tear whose seam
falls on a row boundary is believed, so a group can be recorded printing
the same line twice. Count a group's distinct lines before trusting its
standing.

A group of several pages is watched a page at a time, the space bar
turning each once it has gone quiet or run out its frames, until the
module's menu comes back; its standing names how many pages it showed,
"and more" where the turning stopped at a cap rather than at the menu. A
press that changes nothing is made again, six times in all, for a module
busy with its own work reads no keys; a group of several pages that goes
unanswered after that stands with "then no answer", and one of a single
page stands as it was. A page is counted where a press is followed by any
change on the screen, which a module still drawing the page before can
make itself.

A group marked "cut off at the cap" was still drawing screens this reader
had not seen when a page's budget of frames ran out, where the rest had gone
quiet before theirs did. The mark follows what a group draws rather than
what it grades, so a change to the machine can hand it to one group and
take it from another; arriving or leaving, it is no verdict on either.

A group that names silicon's value only where it differs is worth
knowing about: it grades itself while the machine is wrong and prints its
measurement alone once it is right, so it leaves the tally above by being
agreed with. A group that makes the comparison itself and prints only
its verdict leaves the tally the same way: the word stands while the
machine is wrong and is gone once it is right. A group falling out of
that count is not the same as a group that could not be read.

fails on the first line where the two differ. The screens behind these
standings are in the module records written beside the sweep's own copy.
